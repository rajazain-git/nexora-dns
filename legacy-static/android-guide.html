Android Troubleshooting

Quick fixes for common issues when configuring DNS on Android devices.

Private DNS is a native feature available on Android 9 (Pie) and above. If your phone runs Android 8 or earlier, Private DNS will not be present. Please use the Wi-Fi DNS tab instead. On Samsung phones, look under Settings > Connections > More connection settings. On Xiaomi/MIUI, look under Settings > Connection & Sharing > Private DNS.

Verify that your hostname is entered accurately with no extra spaces or http:// prefixes. Also verify that you have an active internet connection. Some local Wi-Fi networks block port 853 (DNS-over-TLS). If this happens, switch to mobile data or use the static Wi-Fi DNS method.

The manual static IP method only applies to Wi-Fi. To enable low-latency gaming DNS on cellular mobile data (4G/5G), you must configure the Private DNS method (Android 9+). Once set in Private DNS, it automatically applies to all mobile data connections system-wide.

⚠️

How to Remove DNS / Reset to Default

You can restore your Android device back to factory ISP network settings at any time without resetting network configurations:

  1. For Private DNS: Go to Settings > Network & Internet (or Connections) > Private DNS, and switch the selection back to 'Automatic' or 'Off'. Tap Save.
  2. For Wi-Fi DNS: Go to Settings > Wi-Fi, select or modify your network, scroll to IP settings, and switch from 'Static' back to 'DHCP'. Tap Save.
  3. Your Android device will immediately revert to your telecom operator or router's default DNS servers.
